# DORA Article-by-Article Reference (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554)

*Reconstructed reference — regenerated on 2026-09-11 to replace a file missing from the original skill upload; verify against the official texts (EUR-Lex) before relying on citations.*

Complete per-article reference for all 64 articles of Regulation (EU) 2022/2554
(DORA), OJ L 333, 27.12.2022. Application date: **17 January 2025** (Art. 64).
Use this file to confirm article numbers, titles, and headline obligations before
citing them in advice. Where an article is implemented by an RTS/ITS, see
`rts-its-guide.md` for the regulation number and detail.

**Applies to** abbreviations: **FE** = all in-scope financial entities (Art. 2(1)(a)–(t));
**CI/PI/EMI** = credit institutions, payment institutions, e-money institutions (and AISPs);
**TPSP** = ICT third-party service provider; **CTPP** = critical TPSP; **CA** = competent
authority; **ESAs** = EBA, ESMA, EIOPA.

## Chapter I — General Provisions (Art. 1–4)

| Art. | Title | Summary of obligations | Applies to |
|------|-------|------------------------|-----------|
| 1 | Subject matter | Establishes uniform requirements for the security of network and information systems of financial entities: ICT risk management, incident reporting, resilience testing, third-party risk, information sharing, and the CTPP oversight framework. Establishes DORA as lex specialis relative to NIS2 for the financial sector. | Framework provision |
| 2 | Scope | Lists the 21 categories of in-scope entities (Art. 2(1)(a)–(u)), including credit institutions, payment and e-money institutions, investment firms, CASPs, CSDs, CCPs, trading venues, trade repositories, AIFMs, UCITS ManCos, data reporting service providers, (re)insurance undertakings and intermediaries, IORPs, CRAs, benchmark administrators, crowdfunding providers, securitisation repositories — and ICT third-party service providers. Art. 2(3) exemptions include AIFMs under AIFMD Art. 3(2), certain small IORPs (< 15 members), and small insurance intermediaries that are micro/small/medium enterprises. | FE + TPSPs |
| 3 | Definitions | 65 definitions, including "ICT risk" (3(5)), "ICT-related incident" (3(8)), "major ICT-related incident" (3(10)), "cyber threat" (3(12)), "significant cyber threat" (3(13)), "critical or important function" (3(22)), "ICT third-party service provider" (3(19)), "ICT concentration risk" (3(29)), "TLPT" (3(17)). Cite definitions from here, not from NIS2. | Framework provision |
| 4 | Proportionality principle | Chapter II obligations apply proportionately to size, overall risk profile, and the nature, scale and complexity of services. Proportionality does not waive obligations — it calibrates implementation depth. Micro-enterprises benefit from specific carve-outs throughout the text (e.g. Art. 5(4), 6(5), 19(1)). | FE |

## Chapter II — ICT Risk Management (Art. 5–16)

| Art. | Title | Summary of obligations | Applies to |
|------|-------|------------------------|-----------|
| 5 | Governance and organisation | The management body defines, approves, oversees and is **accountable** for the ICT risk management framework (5(2)); sets ICT risk tolerance, approves ICT policies, business continuity and audit plans, budgets, and reviews third-party arrangements; members must maintain sufficient ICT risk knowledge, including through regular training (5(4)). | FE (except Art. 16 entities apply simplified regime) |
| 6 | ICT risk management framework | Maintain a sound, comprehensive, well-documented ICT RMF including strategies, policies, procedures, protocols and tools (6(1)–(2)); assign oversight to an independent control function (6(4)); review at least yearly (micro-enterprises: periodically) and after major incidents, tests or supervisory instructions (6(5)); subject the RMF to internal audit (6(6)); include a digital operational resilience strategy (6(8)). Detailed via CDR (EU) 2024/1774. | FE |
| 7 | ICT systems, protocols and tools | Use and maintain ICT systems, protocols and tools that are appropriate, reliable, technologically resilient, and with sufficient capacity to handle stressed conditions. | FE |
| 8 | Identification | Identify, classify and document all ICT-supported business functions, information assets and ICT assets, and their dependencies (8(1)); identify sources of ICT risk continuously (8(2)); maintain and yearly update inventories of ICT assets (8(4)–(6)); perform risk assessments upon major changes (8(3)); identify all ICT processes dependent on TPSPs (8(5)). | FE |
| 9 | Protection and prevention | Continuously monitor and control ICT security; deploy policies for availability, authenticity, integrity, confidentiality; implement network security management, encryption, strong authentication, access management (least privilege), change management, and patching policies (9(2)–(4)). | FE |
| 10 | Detection | Establish mechanisms to promptly detect anomalous activities, including ICT network performance issues and ICT-related incidents; multiple layers of control, defined alert thresholds and criteria, and automatic alerting for incident-handling staff (10(1)–(2)); DRSPs need systems to check trade report completeness (10(4)). | FE |
| 11 | Response and recovery | Implement a comprehensive ICT business continuity policy (11(1)); dedicated ICT response and recovery plans, tested at least yearly (11(3)–(4), 11(6)); crisis management function and internal/external crisis communication (11(2)(e), 11(7)); BIA-based recovery objectives; keep records of activities during disruptions (11(9)); CCPs/CSDs/trading venues face stricter recovery requirements (11(5)). | FE |
| 12 | Backup policies and procedures, restoration and recovery procedures and methods | Backup policies specifying scope and frequency, plus restoration/recovery procedures (12(1)); backup systems physically and logically segregated from source systems (12(3)); test restoration periodically (12(2)); redundant ICT capacities; CSDs need a secondary processing site (12(5)); micro-enterprises assess restoration cost-benefit only against risk profile. | FE |
| 13 | Learning and evolving | Gather intelligence on vulnerabilities and cyber threats; post-incident reviews after major incidents analysing causes and improvements (13(2)); include lessons in the ICT RMF; monitor technology developments; mandatory ICT security awareness programmes and digital operational resilience training for staff **and management body** (13(6)); monitor relevant TPSP developments. | FE |
| 14 | Communication | Crisis communication plans enabling responsible disclosure of at least major ICT incidents to clients, counterparts and the public as appropriate (14(1)); communication policies for internal staff and external stakeholders (14(2)); at least one designated spokesperson role (14(3)). | FE |
| 15 | Further harmonisation of ICT risk management tools, methods, processes and policies | Mandate for ESAs to develop RTS specifying Art. 9–11 elements (security policies, controls, detection, BCP/response and recovery components). Fulfilled by **CDR (EU) 2024/1774** (Title II). | ESAs (obligation flows to FE via the RTS) |
| 16 | Simplified ICT risk management framework | Small and non-interconnected investment firms, exempted PIs/EMIs, small IORPs and certain other small entities apply a lighter framework: documented ICT RMF, key asset identification, continuity of critical functions, monitoring, incident handling — but not the full Art. 5–15 apparatus. Specified in CDR (EU) 2024/1774 (Title III). | Art. 16(1)-listed entities only |

## Chapter III — ICT-Related Incident Management, Classification and Reporting (Art. 17–23)

| Art. | Title | Summary of obligations | Applies to |
|------|-------|------------------------|-----------|
| 17 | ICT-related incident management process | Define, establish and implement a process to detect, manage and notify ICT-related incidents; record all ICT incidents and significant cyber threats; assign roles, early-warning indicators, escalation paths, classification procedures, and communication plans; report at least major incidents to senior management and inform the management body. | FE |
| 18 | Classification of ICT-related incidents and cyber threats | Classify incidents using criteria in 18(1): clients/counterparts/transactions affected; duration and service downtime (via reputational impact criterion 18(1)(b) and duration 18(1)(c)); geographical spread; data losses (availability, authenticity, integrity, confidentiality); criticality of services affected; economic impact. Classify cyber threats as significant per 18(2). Materiality thresholds set in **CDR (EU) 2024/1772**. See `incident-classification.md`. | FE |
| 19 | Reporting of major ICT-related incidents and voluntary notification of significant cyber threats | Report **major** incidents to the CA in three stages — initial notification, intermediate report, final report (19(4)) — using the timelines/content of CDR (EU) 2025/301 and templates of CIR (EU) 2025/302; may voluntarily notify significant cyber threats (19(2)); inform clients affected by a major incident, and clients potentially affected by a significant cyber threat, of protective measures (19(3)); CAs forward reports to ESAs, ECB, NIS2 CSIRTs as relevant (19(6)). | FE |
| 20 | Harmonisation of reporting content and templates | Mandate for ESAs to develop RTS on content and time limits (fulfilled by CDR (EU) 2025/301) and ITS on standard forms/templates (fulfilled by CIR (EU) 2025/302). | ESAs |
| 21 | Centralisation of reporting of major ICT-related incidents | ESAs, with ECB and ENISA, to prepare a joint report assessing feasibility of a single EU hub for major incident reporting. Feasibility report delivered January 2025; no direct entity obligation. | ESAs |
| 22 | Supervisory feedback | CAs acknowledge receipt and may provide feedback, guidance and anonymised intelligence on similar threats; ESAs publish yearly anonymised aggregate reports on major incidents. No direct entity obligation, but expect supervisory dialogue after reports. | CAs / ESAs |
| 23 | Operational or security payment-related incidents concerning credit institutions, payment institutions, account information service providers and electronic money institutions | Extends Chapter III (Art. 17–22) to **operational or security payment-related incidents**, including non-ICT ones, for the listed payment-sector entities. Replaces PSD2 Art. 96 incident reporting for these entities. | CI, PI, AISP, EMI |

## Chapter IV — Digital Operational Resilience Testing (Art. 24–27)

| Art. | Title | Summary of obligations | Applies to |
|------|-------|------------------------|-----------|
| 24 | General requirements for the performance of digital operational resilience testing | Maintain a sound, comprehensive testing programme as integral part of the ICT RMF; risk-based, proportionate; tests by independent (internal or external) parties; procedures to prioritise and remediate findings; test all ICT systems supporting critical or important functions **at least yearly** (24(6)). Micro-enterprises may combine proportionality considerations. | FE (except micro-enterprises for some elements) |
| 25 | Testing of ICT tools and systems | Programme must provide for: vulnerability assessments and scans, open-source analyses, network security assessments, gap analyses, physical security reviews, questionnaires and scanning software, source code reviews where feasible, scenario-based tests, compatibility testing, performance testing, end-to-end testing, penetration testing (25(1)); CSDs and CCPs must conduct vulnerability assessments before deployments/redeployments of critical applications (25(2)). | FE |
| 26 | Advanced testing of ICT tools, systems and processes based on TLPT | Entities identified per 26(8) carry out **threat-led penetration testing at least every 3 years** on live production systems supporting critical or important functions; TPSPs underpinning those functions are included in scope (26(2)–(3)), with pooled testing possible where provider participation would harm service quality (26(4)); use of internal testers allowed under conditions (26(8) second subpara.); authorities issue an attestation enabling mutual recognition (26(6)–(7)). Specified in CDR (EU) 2025/1190; aligned with TIBER-EU. | Significant FE designated by CAs |
| 27 | Requirements for testers for the carrying out of TLPT | Testers (and threat-intelligence providers) must have suitability, reputation, technical and organisational capability, certifications/adherence to formal codes, professional indemnity insurance; internal testers additionally require CA approval, sufficient dedicated resources, and conflict-of-interest safeguards; threat-intelligence provider must always be external (26(8)(a)). | FE conducting TLPT; testers |

## Chapter V — Managing of ICT Third-Party Risk (Art. 28–44)

### Section I — Key principles (Art. 28–30)

| Art. | Title | Summary of obligations | Applies to |
|------|-------|------------------------|-----------|
| 28 | General principles | Manage ICT third-party risk within the ICT RMF and per the principle of full responsibility of the FE (28(1)); adopt a strategy on ICT third-party risk including a policy on the use of ICT services supporting critical/important functions (28(2), detailed in CDR (EU) 2024/1773); maintain and update the **Register of Information** at entity/sub-consolidated/consolidated level and report it yearly to the CA (28(3), templates in CIR (EU) 2024/2956); pre-contracting assessment including due diligence, concentration risk, and conflicts (28(4)); only contract with TPSPs meeting appropriate information security standards (28(5)); audit rights and inspection cadence (28(6)); defined termination triggers (28(7)); **exit strategies** for ICT services supporting critical/important functions (28(8)). | FE |
| 29 | Preliminary assessment of ICT concentration risk at entity level | When assessing 28(4)(c), weigh: contracting a hardly-substitutable TPSP; multiple arrangements with the same or closely connected TPSPs (29(1)); assess benefits/costs of alternatives; where subcontracting chains for critical/important functions cross into third countries, assess insolvency law, data protection and enforceability implications, and long/complex chains' impact on monitoring ability (29(2)). | FE |
| 30 | Key contractual provisions | Written contracts with full allocation of rights and obligations (30(1)); **30(2)** minimum provisions for **all** ICT service contracts (service description, locations and change notification, data protection incl. CIA and access/return on termination, incident assistance, cooperation with authorities, termination rights, participation in security awareness programmes); **30(3)** additional provisions where the service supports **critical or important functions** (full SLAs with performance targets, reporting obligations, incident reporting, business contingency implementation and participation in TLPT, unrestricted audit/access rights for the FE, CA and resolution authority, exit transition periods and assistance); standard contractual clauses may be considered (30(4)); subcontracting conditions specified by RTS (30(5)) — CDR (EU) 2025/532. See `third-party-risk.md`. | FE and their TPSPs |

### Section II — Oversight Framework of critical ICT third-party service providers (Art. 31–44)

| Art. | Title | Summary of obligations | Applies to |
|------|-------|------------------------|-----------|
| 31 | Designation of critical ICT third-party service providers | ESAs (via the Joint Committee, on Oversight Forum recommendation) designate CTPPs based on: systemic impact of failure, systemic character/importance of client FEs (incl. G-SIIs/O-SIIs), reliance for critical/important functions, and substitutability (31(2)); criteria quantified in CDR (EU) 2024/1502; non-designated TPSPs may opt in (31(11)); FEs may not use TPSPs established in a third country that would be designated critical unless it establishes an EU subsidiary within 12 months (31(12)). | ESAs; TPSPs |
| 32 | Structure of the Oversight Framework | Establishes the **Oversight Forum** as a Joint Committee sub-committee for cross-sector CTPP risk work, benchmarking, and coordination; annual oversight priorities; appoints the Lead Overseer (EBA, ESMA or EIOPA depending on the largest share of client FE assets). | ESAs |
| 33 | Tasks of the Lead Overseer | Lead Overseer assesses whether each CTPP has comprehensive, sound and effective rules, procedures, mechanisms and arrangements to manage ICT risk it may pose to FEs; adopts a yearly individual oversight plan per CTPP. | Lead Overseer; CTPPs |
| 34 | Operational coordination between Lead Overseers | The three Lead Overseers set up a **Joint Oversight Network (JON)** to coordinate approaches, joint methodologies and positions; common oversight protocol. | ESAs |
| 35 | Powers of the Lead Overseer | Powers to: request information and documentation (35(1)(a)), conduct investigations and inspections (35(1)(b)), request reports after oversight activities with remediation measures (35(1)(c)), and issue **recommendations** (35(1)(d)) on ICT security, conditions/terms of service to FEs, subcontracting (including refusal of subcontracting where risks are unmanaged, 35(1)(d)(iv)); periodic penalty payments for non-cooperation (35(6)) up to 1% of average daily worldwide turnover, applied daily up to six months. | Lead Overseer; CTPPs |
| 36 | Exercise of the powers of the Lead Overseer outside the Union | Conditions under which oversight powers (Art. 35, 38, 39) may be exercised on CTPP premises in third countries, requiring administrative arrangements with third-country authorities and CTPP consent frameworks. | Lead Overseer |
| 37 | Request for information | Procedure for information requests to CTPPs by simple request or by decision (with periodic penalty payments attaching to decisions). | Lead Overseer; CTPPs |
| 38 | General investigations | Investigation powers: examine records, data, procedures; obtain copies; summon and question representatives; interview other consenting persons. | Lead Overseer |
| 39 | Inspections | On-site inspection powers at CTPP business premises, including sealing premises/records; prior notification to the CTPP and coordination with the JET; off-site inspections also possible. | Lead Overseer |
| 40 | Ongoing oversight | Oversight conducted with support of a **Joint Examination Team (JET)** per CTPP — staff from ESAs and relevant CAs (composition/tasks in CDR (EU) 2025/420), coordinated by a Lead Overseer staff member. | Lead Overseer; JETs |
| 41 | Harmonisation of conditions enabling the conduct of the oversight activities | Mandate for RTS on: information to be provided in the opt-in application, information in the Art. 35(1)(c) report templates, JET composition criteria, and assessment details — fulfilled by CDR (EU) 2025/295 (harmonisation) and CDR (EU) 2025/420 (JETs). | ESAs |
| 42 | Follow-up by competent authorities | Within 60 days CTPPs must notify intention to follow recommendations or explain (comply-or-explain); Lead Overseer publicly discloses non-compliance where decided; **CAs may, as last resort, require FEs to suspend or terminate** use of a non-compliant CTPP's services (42(6)). | CTPPs; CAs; FEs |
| 43 | Oversight fees | CTPPs pay fees fully covering the Lead Overseer's oversight expenditure; fee amounts and payment modalities in CDR (EU) 2024/1505 (based on applicable turnover). | CTPPs |
| 44 | International cooperation | EBA, ESMA, EIOPA may conclude administrative arrangements with third-country authorities to foster cooperation on ICT third-party risk; joint confidential report on third-country developments every 5 years. | ESAs |

## Chapter VI — Information-Sharing Arrangements (Art. 45)

| Art. | Title | Summary of obligations | Applies to |
|------|-------|------------------------|-----------|
| 45 | Information-sharing arrangements on cyber threat information and intelligence | FEs **may** exchange cyber threat information and intelligence (indicators of compromise, TTPs, alerts, configuration tools) within trusted communities, provided sharing aims to enhance digital operational resilience, occurs within protective arrangements preserving business confidentiality, data protection and competition rules (45(1)); arrangements define participation conditions and involvement of authorities/TPSPs (45(2)); entities notify CAs of participation upon validation of membership and of its cessation (45(3)). Voluntary but supervisors expect a documented position. | FE (voluntary) |

## Chapter VII — Competent Authorities (Art. 46–56)

| Art. | Title | Summary of obligations | Applies to |
|------|-------|------------------------|-----------|
| 46 | Competent authorities | Maps each Art. 2(1) entity category to its CA under the relevant sectoral act (e.g. CRD/SSM for credit institutions, PSD2 CAs for PIs, MiFID II CAs for investment firms, MiCA CAs for CASPs, Solvency II CAs for insurers). Identify your CA from this article. | FE / CAs |
| 47 | Cooperation with structures and authorities established by Directive (EU) 2022/2555 | CAs and NIS2 authorities (incl. CSIRTs, single points of contact) cooperate and exchange information; DORA CAs may consult and share with NIS2 structures. Basis for the DORA–NIS2 interface. | CAs |
| 48 | Cooperation between authorities | CAs, ESAs, and resolution/other authorities cooperate closely, exchange information for their tasks; ESAs and CAs may participate in Oversight Forum activities. | CAs / ESAs |
| 49 | Financial cross-sector exercises, communication and cooperation | ESAs and CAs may build crisis and contingency communication mechanisms and run cross-sector crisis exercises; EU-SCICF-type coordination for systemic cyber incidents. | ESAs / CAs |
| 50 | Administrative penalties and remedial measures | Member States equip CAs with powers: cease-and-desist orders, requiring termination of practices, remedial measures, public notices, administrative penalties per national law; effective, proportionate, dissuasive. DORA sets no fixed EU-wide fine ceilings for FEs — national implementing law determines amounts. | Member States / CAs / FE |
| 51 | Exercise of the power to impose administrative penalties and remedial measures | Factors CAs weigh: materiality/gravity/duration of the breach, responsibility, financial strength, profits gained/losses avoided, third-party losses, cooperation level, previous breaches. | CAs |
| 52 | Criminal penalties | Member States may provide criminal instead of administrative penalties for certain breaches; must notify the Commission/ESAs and ensure cross-border cooperation channels remain. | Member States |
| 53 | Notification duties | Member States notify penalty and criminal provisions to Commission, ESAs by application date and of subsequent amendments. | Member States |
| 54 | Publication of administrative penalties | CAs publish decisions imposing penalties (identity, breach nature) on their websites without undue delay; anonymisation/deferral where publication is disproportionate or jeopardises stability/investigations; publication maintained ≤ 5 years. | CAs |
| 55 | Professional secrecy | Confidential information received under DORA covered by professional secrecy obligations for all persons working for CAs. | CAs |
| 56 | Data protection | ESAs and CAs process personal data only where necessary under DORA, in line with GDPR (2016/679) / EUDPR (2018/1725); retention max 15 years except litigation. | ESAs / CAs |
